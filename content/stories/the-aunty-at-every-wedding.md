Every family has one. Ours is Aunty Rachael, who is not, as far as anyone has been able to establish across three generations of asking, related to us by blood, marriage, or any documentable path. She simply arrived at my cousin's wedding in 2004 wearing better aso-ebi than the actual family, cried during the exchange of vows loud enough to be heard over the choir, and was at every wedding after that.

Nobody invited her the second time either. Nobody has ever needed to. Word travels — a date, a venue, a colour — and Aunty Rachael appears wearing precisely that colour, sometimes better sourced than the bride's own family, and takes up a front-row seat with the calm authority of someone who paid for it.

She cried at my sister's wedding. She cried, four years later, at my sister's very quiet, very private divorce settlement dinner, which technically was not an event she should have known about, let alone attended, and yet there she was, dabbing her eyes with the same handkerchief, saying "God will do it" to a room that was mostly just tired lawyers.

Nobody has ever asked her to leave. That's the part outsiders can't understand when I tell this story. It's not that we're too polite. It's that somewhere in the last twenty years, Aunty Rachael's tears became load-bearing. She cries so the rest of us don't have to perform crying we don't feel yet, or can't afford to feel in public. She is doing a job. Nobody assigned it. Nobody pays her. She simply does it, faithfully, at every gain and every loss the family has, whether she is family or not.

Last month my youngest cousin got engaged, and within the hour there was a message in the family group chat, from an aunty three degrees removed, asking only: "Does Rachael know yet?" As if the engagement wasn't real, wasn't fully alive, until she did.

She'll be at the wedding. Front row. Better dressed than the bride's mother, probably. Crying before the couple even reaches the altar. And somehow, every single time, it will be exactly what the room needed, from a woman none of us can explain and none of us would dream of doing this without.

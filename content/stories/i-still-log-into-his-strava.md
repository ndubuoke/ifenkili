---
title: I Still Log Into His Strava
excerpt: We haven't spoken in three years. I know exactly how far he ran this morning.
category: confessions
anonymous: true
date: 2026-09-14
mood: Uneasy
---

It started as a normal thing two people do. He got into running the year we moved in together, and I followed his Strava because that's what you do, you give kudos, you comment "beast" under a half marathon, it means nothing, it's a thumbs up with extra steps.

We broke up three years ago. Badly enough that we don't have mutual friends anymore, badly enough that I changed my route to the gym so I wouldn't pass his old flat. I blocked him everywhere it was easy to block him. I did not, somehow, ever get around to unfollowing an app that tells me when he runs.

So I know things I have no right to know. I know he runs at 6:40am now, not 7, which means something changed in his mornings — a new job, a new person setting the pace of the house, I don't ask myself which. I know he ran eleven kilometres on what would have been our anniversary, further than his usual, and I have spent more time than I'd like to admit deciding whether that means anything at all, or whether a man just felt like running further one Tuesday.

I don't comment. I haven't given kudos in three years. I just open the app sometimes, the way you press on a bruise to check if it still hurts, and it always still hurts, a little, in a duller way each time, which I've decided to call progress.

I know this is not a healthy way to grieve a person who is, by every account, fine without me. I know the honest move is to unfollow, and that I could do it in four seconds, and that the only reason I haven't is that some small, humiliating part of me doesn't want to lose the last thread, even a thread this thin, even a thread that only tells me pace and distance and never anything that matters.

Someday I'll do it. Not today. Today he ran 7k in light rain, and I am, against every instinct I have, glad to know he's still out there moving.

title: The Apartment We Never Signed For

We found it on a Tuesday, on an app neither of us paid for, and by Wednesday we had a name for it that wasn't the postcode. The second bedroom would be his desk. The kitchen window faced a wall, but the estate agent called it "characterful," and we laughed about that for a week.

We didn't have the deposit yet. We had a plan for the deposit, which felt, at the time, close enough.

I picked a paint colour for a room that wasn't ours — a green like the inside of a bottle — and sent it to him with no context, and he replied with a paint colour of his own, a deep ochre for the hallway that didn't exist. We furnished a flat we didn't have with things we hadn't bought. That was most of that autumn.

The landlord went with a couple who could pay six months up front. Nobody's fault. We said "next time" the way you say it when you both already suspect there won't be a next time, not because you've stopped wanting it, but because wanting it that hard once seems to use something up.

We stayed together four more months, in our separate flats, on our separate sides of the river. Nothing dramatic ended it. There was no reply-all, no scene, no line either of us can point to. We just stopped mentioning the ochre hallway. Then we stopped mentioning much at all.

I still see the colour sometimes — on a door, in a paint shop window, once on a jumper on the tube — and I feel the specific, useless grief of missing a home I never lived in, with a person I no longer speak to. Not the breakup. The apartment. The idea of the two of us being the kind of people who choose paint together.

He's engaged now, to someone else, in a flat I've never seen. I hope the kitchen window looks at something better than a wall. I hope, if it doesn't, she still calls it characterful, and means it, and it's still true five years later.

That's the part I got wrong, I think. Not the ending. The believing that picking a colour together was the same thing as choosing each other. It wasn't nothing. It just wasn't enough.

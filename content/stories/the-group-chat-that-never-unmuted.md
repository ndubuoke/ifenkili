title: The Group Chat That Never Unmuted
excerpt: A two-minute story about the friend group chat that went silent the year everyone got busy, and the one message that finally broke it.

There were nine of us in the chat at its peak, back when it had a name involving a bad pun and a group photo none of us would still use. For four years it never slept — memes at 2am, breakup updates in real time, an entire wedding planned inside it, badly.

Then, slowly, the way these things go, it thinned out. Not a falling out. Just jobs, cities, a couple of babies, one divorce nobody quite knew how to reply to so nobody did. The chat went from forty messages a day to four a week to, eventually, the little grey "typing…" that would appear and vanish without ever becoming a message, three or four times a month, from someone who'd thought better of whatever they were about to say into a room that had gone quiet.

I muted it eventually. Not out of anger. Out of the specific guilt of a notification you no longer know how to answer.

Then, seven months of silence in, somebody posted just a photo. No caption. A picture of the actual physical booth we used to sit in at the old pub, which had, apparently, finally closed down — a "For Lease" sign taped inside the window where our table used to be.

Every single person in that chat replied within the hour. Not with jokes this time. With actual sentences. Someone said they still had the receipt from the last time we were all there together, kept in a drawer for no reason they could explain until that exact moment. Someone else admitted they'd nearly left the group twice and hadn't, because leaving felt more final than staying silent.

We didn't become the chat we used to be. Nobody expected that, and pretending otherwise would have been its own kind of lie. But we made a plan — a real one, with an actual date — to meet at whatever pub replaces that one, and for the first time in over a year, somebody typed "can't wait" and meant it enough to send it.

It turns out a group chat doesn't die when it goes quiet. It just waits for something small enough to be sad about together, and honest enough to be worth breaking the silence for.

---
title: Two Weeks, Read Aloud
excerpt: HR asked me to read my resignation letter out loud on the call, so everyone could hear I meant it.
category: corporate
anonymous: true

---

I wrote the letter the way you're supposed to — three sentences, no reasons given, "grateful for the opportunities," the works. I sent it to my manager at 8:58am, before the standup, so I wouldn't have to say it to his face first.

He forwarded it to HR before I'd finished my coffee. HR forwarded it back to me with a calendar invite titled "Quick Chat — 15 mins," which is corporate for something that will not take fifteen minutes and will not feel quick.

On the call there were three of them and one of me, which already tells you what kind of chat it was. The HR lead — someone I had exchanged maybe four sentences with in two years — asked me, gently, professionally, unbelievably, to read the letter out loud. "Just so we've all heard it in your own words," she said, like my own words hadn't already arrived in an email with a timestamp.

So I read it. Three sentences, into a laptop microphone, to two people paid to make sure I meant it and one manager who wouldn't meet the camera. It felt like being asked to confess to a crime that was, technically, my legal right.

Afterwards they asked if I'd "reconsider with the right package," which is corporate for we don't want to explain the headcount hole to the board this quarter. I said no. They asked again, differently. I said no, differently. Eventually someone said "understood" in the tone people use when they very much do not understand, and the call ended four minutes over its fifteen.

I don't think about the job much anymore. I think about the reading-aloud part more than I'd like to admit — the strange theatre of it, the way a decision I'd already made, alone, at my kitchen table, at 6am, needed a live performance before three witnesses to become real to them.

I've told the story at two dinner parties since. It gets a laugh both times. It only stopped feeling like an insult somewhere around the second telling, when I realised the real punchline: it was never quick, and it was never really about hearing me. It was about making sure I heard myself.
